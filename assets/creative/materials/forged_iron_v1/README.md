# Forged Iron v1

`forged_iron_v1` is the quiet, intact face of hand-forged architectural iron.
It is the reusable core for straps, hinges, hasps, rings, nails, and other
hardware. Rust, pits, scratches, edge loss, soot, dirt, and damage do not belong
to this material. Those are placement-dependent overlays and later library
assets.

The package contains:

- four repeat-safe, hand-authored face-pattern variations;
- metre-valued component coordinates that survive object resizing and assembly;
- twelve node-ready component atlases separating scale and exposed-iron colour,
  physical response, finite tooling, compact-scale, micro, and worked normals,
  three metre-height frequencies, worked response, and semantic masks;
- compatibility composites for inspection and targets that cannot consume the
  component lanes;
- nine reusable Blender shader groups, two physical Principled responses, one
  four-band whiteout normal-combination path, a named worked tangent, and one
  fabrication-aware metre-valued displacement path;
- no generic Noise or Voronoi node in the material;
- a reusable Geometry Nodes family for four authored nail and rivet profiles;
- independent, default-off oxidation and contact-polish controls;
- an exact-width and exact-length strap proof with a manifold faceted square
  nail, manifold low domed rivet, calibrated cylinder, and smooth sphere;
- a realized copy of the current structural-oak door for compatibility;
- the approved eight-piece, 4.06 m openwork hinge as the actual acceptance
  target, with clay, material, grazing, base-colour, and worked-response proofs.

No image in the material was generated by AI.

## What the door reference actually shows

The primary visual reference is the front view of
[door 55.61.170 at The Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/468492).
It is not a flat sheet of brown metal. Its photograph combines:

1. broad iron faces;
2. compact red-brown surface alteration;
3. exposed brown wood seen through the scrollwork;
4. deep cast shadows made by raised iron;
5. occlusion under overlapping iron members;
6. photographic highlights and colour adaptation.

Those causes can reach nearly the same photographed RGB value. Copying all dark
brown pixels into base colour would bake the door's lighting, its wood, and its
depth into the iron.

The reference audit therefore classifies every sampled point before allowing it
to influence the material:

| Class | Count | Permitted use |
|---|---:|---|
| confident broad iron face | 11 | hue relationship only |
| plausible compact oxidation | 3 | optional oxidation-overlay palette only |
| shadow or material ambiguous | 6 | excluded |

The complete point-by-point decision record is
`references/met_468492_front_iron_palette_samples.json`. Even the eleven
accepted pixels are not copied as PBR base colour. They establish relative
blue-black, graphite, violet-black, and warm-black relationships after the
photographic illumination has been removed.

The secondary
[door 25.120.291](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/472341)
supports only the large-value and strapwork hierarchy. It does not add a second
photographic noise field.

## Measured scale

Two separately catalogued Met straps anchor the material to real stock:

| Object | Date | Length | Width | Role |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| [55.61.63](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/475522) | 15th–16th century | 0.349 m | 0.038 m | narrow stock reference |
| [55.61.58](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/475521) | 15th–16th century | 0.406 m | 0.041 m | acceptance specimen |

The proof strap is exactly `0.406 m × 0.041 m` in the two catalogued axes. Its
`0.006 m` thickness is only a display thickness because the museum record does
not supply that dimension. It is labeled as unmeasured in the profile and must
not become a production-stock claim.

A measured
[Colonial Williamsburg forging hammer 2020-20,16](https://emuseum.history.org/objects/107709/forging-hammer)
provides later-period tool envelopes:

- main face: `41.275 × 31.75 mm`;
- cross-peen face: `36.5125 × 12.7 mm`.

These measurements bound the authored events. They do not assert that the
medieval iron was made with that exact hammer, or that every blow printed its
complete face. Marks are allowed to be smaller, softer, incomplete, clustered,
overlapped, or absent.

## Pattern authoring

The champion pattern is a physical field measuring `1.218 × 0.164 m`. That
length is three times the measured acceptance strap and its width is four times
the measured strap width. It contains:

- ten broad colour fields;
- twenty-six finite planishing or cross-peen events;
- twenty-three finite compact-scale tone plates;
- ten finite, band-limited micro modes;
- long quiet intervals with no named event;
- four bounded variations selected by `sinc_seed`.

Worked-face direction uses a separate `2.436 × 0.492 m` authored field: twice
the base length and three times the base width. Twelve passes are placed at
irregular longitudinal and cross-stock intervals, with overlaps and quiet
gaps. The separate coordinate preserves 2.4-4.8 mm bundle rhythm while
preventing the tidy repeated rows that appeared when finish and scale shared
the smaller tile.

The source recipe is
`patterns/forged_iron_face_champion_v1.json`. It is deliberately readable and
editable. A material artist can move an event in metres, rotate it in degrees,
change its strength, remove it, or author another quiet interval. The Python
compiler then produces the atlas rather than inventing a new composition.

Variation is bounded to preserve the design:

- position jitter is at most `6 mm` longitudinally and `4 mm` across stock;
- rotation jitter is at most `8°`;
- strength changes remain between `0.86×` and `1.12×`;
- no more than two events may disappear from a variation.

That creates siblings rather than unrelated random textures. The planishing and
cross-peen layouts remain recognizable as the same authored surface family,
while adjacent straps do not repeat pixel-for-pixel.

Red is planishing-face influence, green is cross-peen influence, and blue is
compact-scale plate-tone influence. Quiet is derived as one minus the maximum
of red and green. These are material meanings, not generic grayscale
decoration, so Unreal can preserve or replace each response independently.

The scale plates do not become black spots. They overlap a continuous
blue-black scale ground and can change its tone by at most `28%`. This is the
important visual distinction between an authored forged skin and a leopard-like
procedural scatter. The plates organize subtle value, roughness, and height
transitions inside one surface family; they do not punch holes through to
silver metal.

## Surface scale and what height means

The generator uses a measured modern hot-forged surface as a micro-roughness
proxy: `Ra 6.4 μm`, standard deviation `2.0 μm`, measured with a `2.5 mm`
cutoff. This is explicitly not medieval metrology. It gives the script a
documented micro-scale starting point without claiming that one scalar Ra value
describes a historical surface.

The material does not flatten those scales into one opaque normal image. The
compiler exports three independently encoded 16-bit height lanes:

| Frequency | Physical range | Visual responsibility |
|---|---:|---|
| macro hammer plane | `−85…+85 μm` | finite, broad planishing and cross-peen face changes |
| compact-scale lip | `0…+20 μm` | raised boundary response inside the forge-scale skin |
| micro surface | `−32…+32 μm` | the measured-proxy fine surface |

Their sum is bounded by the compatibility envelope of `−180…+180 μm`. Each
height lane also produces its own OpenGL tangent-space normal. The shader
decodes, scales, and whiteout-combines the three normals rather than averaging
RGB values. Macro, scale-lip, and micro defaults are `1.0`, `0.78`, and `0.52`;
the combined result then passes through one Normal Map node with a restrained
global strength of `0.85`.

Only the micro normal and micro height fade with camera distance: full through
`0.65 m`, smoothly reduced, and absent after `4.5 m`. Macro hammer planes and
scale lips remain because they establish the material's gameplay-distance
read. The three height lanes are decoded back to metres and connected to the
Blender material's Displacement socket. That connection is real, even though
visible mesh displacement still depends on renderer and mesh subdivision.

The authored face masks organize subtle plane and roughness changes; they do
not carve the silhouette. Large forging distortion, rolled edges, necking,
upsetting, punched holes, and bent strap profiles belong in geometry.

## Colour, roughness, and metalness

The base material represents compact dark forge scale, not polished bare iron.
It uses sixteen coordinated dark shades so a face can contain broad cool and
warm passages without turning into a single swatch or an orange rust texture.
Roughness stays in the `0.34–0.82` envelope, but most of the continuous scale
field sits near the rough end. Only the strongest centres of the finite hammer
events become smoother.

Metalness is binary by physical layer:

- the continuous compact scale ground and optional brown oxide: dielectric,
  `0`;
- separately authored contact-exposed bare iron: conductor, `1`.

Grey pixels are permitted only at filtered boundaries between those physical
layers. In this intact candidate, the contact-exposure mask defaults to exactly
zero everywhere. Hammering changes planes and reflection; it never changes
material identity or produces bright silver stamps.

The bare-iron conductor colour is the Epic reference value
`(0.560, 0.570, 0.580)` in linear space. Both `Brown Oxidation Amount` and
`Contact Polish Amount` default to `0.0`; the clean core does not guess where a
hand, latch, floor, rain path, or damaged edge should alter the surface.

The twelve shader inputs have separate jobs:

| Component atlas | Meaning |
|---|---|
| scale colour | de-lit dielectric compact-scale hue |
| iron colour | conductive exposed-iron reflectance |
| layer response RGB | scale roughness, iron roughness, exposed-iron layer mask |
| macro normal | finite hammer-plane tangent response |
| scale normal | compact-scale lip tangent response |
| micro normal | fine measured-proxy response, distance faded |
| worked normal | finite directional luster response with no damage-height claim |
| macro height | signed finite hammer-plane height |
| scale height | positive compact-scale lip height |
| micro height | signed fine height, distance faded |
| worked response RGB | finite coverage, encoded direction, protected rest |
| semantic masks RGB | planishing, cross-peen, scale-plate tone |

The generator also writes combined base-colour, roughness, metalness, normal,
and height maps. Those are compatibility and audit products. The Blender
material deliberately consumes the twelve separated component atlases so their
responses remain independently controllable.

## Geometry and shader coordinate contract

The shader does not infer scale from an object's post-assembly bounding box.
Geometry emits:

- `sinc_iron_u_m`: longitudinal component distance in metres;
- `sinc_iron_v_m`: cross-stock component distance in metres;
- `sinc_seed`: integer atlas variation;
- `sinc_iron_edge_mask`: authored contact/edge lane.
- `sinc_iron_fabrication_scale`: tool-making scale, `1` for the measured strap
  and `10` for the giant hinge;
- `IGGY_IronUV`: the component-local tangent frame for worked reflection.

The shader reconstructs three atlas vectors. Macro tool lanes divide the metre
coordinates by fabrication scale, so the giant hinge receives ten-times tool
footprints. Scale colour, scale lips, micro-topography, and worked finish keep
raw metre coordinates, while worked finish uses its larger independent period,
so the material itself does not inflate into centimetre flakes or repeat in
tidy brush rows. Because coordinates are authored per component, assembly
transforms do not stretch the surface.

For the current acceptance door, the builder realizes the evaluated Geometry
Nodes result into a proof copy, finds each connected iron component, and writes
the two metre attributes in the component's principal axes. The approved wood
material and all pre-existing wood attributes remain untouched. Production
geometry should emit the same attributes before assembly; the realization pass
is an acceptance adapter, not a second material-authoring route.

## Reusable shader-node architecture

The public material group is
`IGGY_SH_ReferenceForgedIron_v003`; callers do not need to know how the surface
is assembled. Its eight dependencies each own one reason to change:

| Node group | Ownership |
|---|---|
| `IGGY_SH_IronCoordinates_v004` | fabrication-scaled macro, world-metre surface, larger non-grid worked coordinates, variation row, and authored edge lane |
| `IGGY_SH_AuthoredIronLanes_v004` | the only image-sampling boundary; exactly twelve texture nodes across macro, surface, and worked vectors |
| `IGGY_SH_ForgeScaleLayer_v002` | dielectric scale colour, roughness, and plate-tone response |
| `IGGY_SH_HammerPlanes_v003` | finite planish, peen, quiet, and roughness response without conductor exposure |
| `IGGY_SH_IronMicroSurface_v002` | camera-distance fade for micro normal |
| `IGGY_SH_WorkedIronSurface_v001` | finite worked coverage, direction, protected rest, normal, and anisotropy |
| `IGGY_SH_NormalCombine_v003` | independent four-frequency strengths and three-stage whiteout combination |
| `IGGY_SH_SurfaceHeight_v003` | 16-bit decode, fabrication-scaled macro height, world-scale scale/micro height, and physical sum |

The public group mixes two actual Principled responses. Compact forge scale is
a rough dielectric with metalness `0`; exposed iron is a conductor with
metalness `1`. The authored exposure mask chooses between them. This matters:
one grey-metal shader with a roughness texture cannot reproduce a dielectric
scale film sitting above conductive iron.

Brown oxidation and contact polish remain independent, default-off controls.
They do not share ownership with the intact forged face, and nothing in the
node architecture decides where a hand, hinge bearing, rain path, or damage
event occurred.

## Blender to Unreal translation boundary

The authored data is engine-neutral; the Blender node graph is not assumed to
be an Unreal shader. The intended Unreal material-function mapping is:

| Blender owner | Unreal owner |
|---|---|
| metre coordinates and seed attributes | custom UV/material-function inputs or explicit mesh attributes |
| authored-lane sampler | texture parameters, with non-colour sampling on response, normal, height, and mask lanes |
| scale and exposed-iron responses | two Material Attributes branches blended by the exposure mask |
| whiteout normal combination | angle-corrected/whiteout normal material function |
| camera-distance micro fade | camera-to-pixel distance derived from `PixelDepth` or world-position distance |
| metre height | a named height input whose consumer is selected per Unreal mesh and platform |

Height parity is intentionally unresolved rather than faked. Conventional
World Position Offset moves existing vertices and therefore depends on mesh
density. Nanite tessellation can consume displacement but is experimental and
platform-sensitive. Parallax occlusion is another possible presentation route,
not a guaranteed equivalent of Blender displacement. Until an Unreal material,
imported test mesh, and target-platform render pass are checked side by side,
the build manifest records `unreal_runtime_parity_verified: false`.

The boundary follows Epic's current documentation for
[depth expressions](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/depth-material-expressions-in-unreal-engine),
[utility material expressions](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/utility-material-expressions-in-unreal-engine),
and
[Nanite-enabled content](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/working-with-naniteenabled-content).
Those pages establish available Unreal inputs and constraints; they do not
constitute parity proof for this material.

## Forged fastener geometry

The fastener pass treats the two door faces as different construction records.
The reverse has six large nails bearing on the diagonal timber braces. The
front has smaller repeated rivets bearing on iron straps and the ring
backplate. Dark wood checks, cast shadows, and the points of nails emerging
through the opposite face are not counted as additional heads.

The hero rose-head proof is dimensioned from
[Met door nail 55.61.134](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/468488):

- complete envelope: `74 × 75 × 156 mm`;
- installed head envelope: `74 × 75 × 17 mm`;
- museum-catalogued dimensions are reproduced exactly;
- the uncatalogued shank cross-section is explicitly authored rather than
  presented as measured.

The reusable `IGGY_GN_ForgedFasteners_v001` group contains four distinct
profiles:

| Family | Section | Construction reading |
|---|---|---|
| rose head | square tapered | four dominant hammer planes forming a shallow, displaced pyramid |
| faceted square peen | square tapered | broad bearing facets and a compact off-centre crown |
| domed rivet | round faceted | controlled low elliptical rings, not a UV-sphere button |
| flattened peen | round faceted | a broad low head with a restrained elliptical finish |

Every profile is built from authored axial rings. Each head has intentional
plane changes, a bearing edge, a crown transition, a complete shank, and a
drawn point. There is no Noise, Voronoi, random dent field, corrosion, or
damage displacement in the geometry.

The bearing surface is also role-specific:

- timber receives the head only; crushed fibres, dark halos, and radial splits
  belong to a later contact-placement pass;
- iron plate may receive a low punched-and-drifted swelling controlled by the
  `Punched Seat` input;
- the swelling is not a washer and does not invent a split around every hole.

The node group uses a bearing-plane origin: the head is above local zero and
the shank extends below it. The donor door's six brace points predated that
contract and were located at the centre of the old shank. The build therefore
adds one explicit `105 mm` origin adapter to those six points before replacing
the obsolete procedural group. Hinge and backplate points already use the
bearing plane and receive no offset. This keeps the correction visible and
prevents every future fastener asset from inheriting a door-specific
compatibility assumption.

## Openwork strap-hinge geometry gate

The Met 55.61.58 hinge has a frozen pre-geometry dossier at
`references/hinges/OPENWORK_STRAP_HINGE_55_61_58_DOSSIER.md` and a
machine-checked recipe at
`profiles/openwork_strap_hinge_55_61_58_decomposition_v1.json`.

The additional museum photograph corrects the earlier GH-018 reading:

- `0.406 m` is the complete two-leaf hinge length, not the moving-leaf length;
- the long leaf is one connected five-cell openwork web, not two sine ribbons;
- negative lancet, flank-leaf, junction-petal, and rail-lune apertures control
  the pattern;
- alternating short knuckles form the pivot;
- the pointed pierced diamond is the tail terminal;
- leaf line work comes from named width, bevel, crown, and face-level stations.

One cubic Bézier quarter is the only unique repeated curve. Five aperture roles
derive from it through declared mirror, scale, shear, rotation, and clipping
operations. The next geometry script remains blocked until that visible
decomposition is reviewed. No replacement `.blend` or render belongs to this
gate.

The reviewed script candidate is
`build_openwork_strap_hinge_geometry_v1.py`. Its default route is a
dependency-free planning audit:

```sh
python3 \
  assets/creative/materials/forged_iron_v1/build_openwork_strap_hinge_geometry_v1.py \
  --plan-only
```

That command validates the complete envelope, fixed/moving ownership, 45
moving-leaf apertures, five-cell spacing, two end pads, terminal bore, and
five-part alternating knuckle schedule. It writes no file.

The Blender geometry route is deliberately double-gated. It requires both an
explicit `--build` argument and `geometry_build_authorized: true` in the
reviewed profile. The geometry compiler still contains no camera, light, or
render operation.

After the saved mesh reopened and passed the topology, scale, aperture,
ownership, and semantic-lane gates, the user separately authorized a
five-view neutral-clay proof. That presentation-only route lives in
`render_openwork_strap_hinge_clay_proof_v1.py`; it reopens the frozen geometry,
hashes it before and after presentation, and refuses geometry mutation,
surface detail, or the forged-iron shader. Its views are reference front,
oblique depth, cell macro, pivot macro, and rear construction. The approved
geometry is now the actual-target input to this material build; it is not
remade or silently simplified by the material script.

```sh
/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender \
  --background \
  --python \
  assets/creative/materials/forged_iron_v1/render_openwork_strap_hinge_clay_proof_v1.py \
  -- --render
```

## Build

```sh
/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender \
  --background \
  --python assets/creative/materials/forged_iron_v1/build_forged_iron_v1.py
```

The build performs four operations:

1. compile the authored JSON recipe into twelve component atlases,
   compatibility composites, and one causal pattern chapter;
2. create the nine-group material architecture using exactly twelve image
   nodes, two Principled physical layers, one four-band whiteout Normal Map
   path, one named worked tangent, and one fabrication-aware displacement path;
3. apply it to the measured strap, proof nail and rivet, calibrated cylinder,
   smooth sphere, realized door hardware, and the approved eight-piece
   openwork hinge;
4. pack the images, render proof passes, validate the contract, and save the
   `.blend`.

Generated outputs are written under `output/`. The validation manifest records
source hashes, dimensions, sample-audit counts, node counts, packed-image
status, manifold proof topology, geometry attributes, default overlay values,
and proof hashes. The proof set contains:

- the actual referenced door in game-like light;
- a neutral five-object material view containing flat, faceted, cylindrical,
  and smoothly curved calibration surfaces;
- a grazing material view;
- a gameplay-distance view that proves micro detail does not become noise;
- emissive base-colour, roughness, metalness, and semantic-mask passes;
- emissive combined-normal and metre-height passes.
- full neutral-clay and live-material views of the actual openwork hinge;
- an actual-hinge grazing view plus base-colour and worked-response diagnostics.

The emissive passes are not beauty renders. They isolate the authored data so a
bright reflection, dark room, or bevel highlight cannot masquerade as texture
quality.

Build the fastener family after the core forged-iron material:

```sh
/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender \
  --background \
  --python assets/creative/materials/forged_iron_v1/build_forged_fasteners_v1.py
```

This second build replaces `SINC_GN_ForgedFasteners_v003`, binds the measured
family to the door's brace, hinge, and pull-plate callers, renders both door
faces and two close-up proofs, then saves
`output/forged_fasteners_v1.blend`.

## Acceptance boundary

This version accepts only the intact forged face:

- real-stock dimensions control visible scale;
- tool events are finite and hand placed;
- scale plates change tone inside a continuous scale skin instead of appearing
  as black spots on raw silver iron;
- quiet surface occupies most of the material;
- the door photograph is segmented before colour use;
- no shadows or wood are baked into iron base colour;
- no generic hammer noise constructs the pattern;
- hammer activity never creates exposed-metal islands;
- giant-house fabrication scale enlarges tool events without enlarging oxide,
  micro, or worked-finish structure;
- metalness, roughness, height, and normal remain independent authored lanes;
- the actual openwork hinge, not a generic swatch, is the acceptance target;
- no rust, pits, scratches, dents, soot, blood, dirt, or damage are active;
- the already-approved structural oak remains unchanged.

Broad strap silhouettes, true plate-hole cuts, hinge knuckle joinery,
wood-contact deformation, oxidation placement, contact-polish placement, and
damage remain separate capability passes.
