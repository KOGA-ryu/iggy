# World Asset 001 — quality review

## Current verdict

The tenth repair build is technically complete and ready for user clay review.
It is not self-declared art-approved. No wood colour, roughness, or photographic
detail was used to hide weak form.

The master now reads as one subtly tapered and bowed structural timber rather
than a displaced cube. The four faces carry independent broad target-plane
passes with independent diagonal sweep directions. The strongest remaining
visual risk is restraint: at gameplay distance the neutral face still reads
cleaner than a hero historic timber. That is preferable to the rejected
alternatives—soft procedural pillows, a visible sampling grid, routed slots,
and repeated parallelogram stamps—but it must be judged against the user's
target.

## Strengths designed into the source

- The object is one continuous timber volume with four hewn faces.
- The geometry owns broad dressed planes; the profile preserves measured local
  tool observations for a later striation lane.
- Taper, bow, crook, twist, and arris change are measured separately.
- End checks begin on cut ends and taper inward.
- Knot sockets are real relief with separate editable knot bodies.
- Joinery areas are explicitly reserved in metres.
- The clean source is preserved before all intrinsic anatomy.
- The evaluated master must remain manifold.

## Failure conditions to inspect

- If the future diagonal striation lane becomes a soft pillow, it has been
  promoted into depth when it belongs in fine normal or linework.
- If the faces resemble noisy terrain, the baseline drift or the number of
  overlapping marks is too high.
- If the beam reads as a beveled cube, the silhouette deviations are too weak.
- If it reads as rotten driftwood, checks or edge losses are too strong.
- If the end close-up resembles a starburst decal, check widths or radial
  regularity are too strong.
- If knots resemble inserted coins, their socket irregularity, recession, or
  future grain influence is insufficient.
- If protected joinery zones look artificially blank, the 24 percent depth
  reduction is too abrupt and needs a feathered boundary.
- If the top, bottom, front, and back could be mirrored without detection, the
  face authorship is insufficient.

## Rendered comparison

### What passed

- The silhouette has a continuous 12 mm bow, 7 mm crook, end-to-end taper, and
  independent arris runs. It does not rely on material contrast.
- Front, back, top, and bottom are not mirrors. Their target-plane anchors,
  tool direction, event density, and cross-face tilts differ.
- The accepted surface uses broad target-plane changes rather than isolated
  local cavities.
- Front/back plane transitions travel in opposing diagonal directions; top and
  bottom have quieter independent sweeps.
- All five checks begin on an exposed end. Their centre paths wobble, their two
  walls are asymmetric, and their apertures narrow into the beam.
- Both edge losses remain rare enough that long clean arris runs dominate.
- Both knot sockets are visible as recessed geometry, but restrained enough
  that future grain deflection remains the primary knot read.
- The evaluated master is closed and manifold after all nine Booleans, bevel,
  and weighted normals.

### What remains at risk

- Neutral front lighting hides much of the 1–3 mm dressed-plane relief. This
  is physically plausible, but the final painterly shader must provide sparse
  diagonal striation and tool-profile linework without inventing cavities.
- The knot bodies are intentionally quiet in clay. If the future grain field
  is weak, they may read as shallow sockets instead of branch anatomy.
- End checks have convincing paths but no annual-ring context yet. The end
  grain material must route rings around them without painting the cavities
  black.
- The 13-pass face rhythm is authored but still regular enough to inspect again
  after the wood material establishes longitudinal scale.

## Repair-pass count

10.

1. Replaced collapsing non-manifold edge-chip cutters with guaranteed-manifold
   convex cutters.
2. Rejected universal smooth shading after it produced pillow-like marks.
3. Rejected universal flat shading after it exposed the mesh as a pixel grid;
   raised the source to 145 by 72 section/perimeter sampling and authored a
   sharp-edge lane.
4. Rebuilt straight end-check wedges as asymmetric, narrowing ribbon paths and
   removed U-shaped sharp borders from the hewing events.
5. Added 13 independent target-plane anchors per face, overlapped neighbouring
   removals, and reduced final depth to the neutral-clay range.
6. Translated the written Scrape/Multiplane Scrape and Flatten workflow into
   literal plane fitting; rejected the smooth proof because it still averaged
   local marks into soft dents.
7. Rejected flat facet normals after the longitudinal grid appeared as a
   staircase under grazing light.
8. Prototyped closed axe-wedge Booleans; the first combined-volume version was
   rejected by the modifier-by-modifier topology gate before save.
9. Separated all 57 wedges into manifold events, then rejected the valid clay
   result because repeated parallelograms read as routed decorative stamps.
10. Kept only the broad dressed planes in geometry, gave each face an
    independent 85–130 mm diagonal sweep, and reduced the Boolean bevel from
    2.6 mm to 0.45 mm. The 57 observations now feed later striation intent.

## Remaining material-only details

- Longitudinal white-oak fibre and growth-ring hierarchy.
- Roughly twenty related brown, grey, ochre, and cool shadow shades within each
  individual face.
- Broad warm/cool passages that blend through the beam rather than assigning
  one colour per plank.
- Knot-driven grain deflection inside the measured influence radii.
- Independent end-grain rings, pores, and cut-face colour.
- Fine sharp broad-axe edge linework.
- Fine fibre normal and restrained height.
- Roughness authored independently after the explicit no-roughness geometry
  acceptance stage.
- Optional soot, wetness, contact polish, and scene damage as separate overlay
  libraries.

Nothing in this list is permission for the material to invent more dents,
checks, edge loss, or universal noise.
