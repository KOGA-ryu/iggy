### The chronological list: the dated backbone of the whole corpus

This is the most useful thing in the two volumes, because it dates everything else. **VERIFIED** on the page images at `dollman_v2` leaves 26 (ENGLISH) and 27 (SCOTTISH), and it is not where a reader would look for it: leaf 24 ends the introductory essay, leaf 25 is genuinely blank, leaves 26–27 carry the list, and leaf 28 begins volume 2's descriptions.

Both columns are headed `DATE.` over an italic `Circa.` — so **every date is a stylistic attribution by two architects in 1863, not a documentary date.** Use them as a relative *ordering*, which is what they are good for.

81 entries in named typological classes: 47 English (8 bede houses and hospitals, 14 mansions, 9 timber houses, 3 chapels, 3 windows, 1 doorway, 2 fireplaces, 3 furniture, 2 panelling, 2 ironwork) and 34 Scottish (9 mansions, 8 windows, 6 doorways, 2 arcading, 5 fireplaces, 4 ungrouped below a short rule). Every date was re-read against the images and every one is transcribed correctly, including three the OCR had dropped or garbled — Higham Ferrers 1430, Norborough 1335 and Chiddingstone 1637 are all genuinely printed — plus the printed oddities `1350 (?)` for the Penshurst bench ends and the braced `Ironwork, various { 16th and 17th Centuries.`

**The period ladder for a generator, English:**

| Style band | Dated exemplars illustrated in this work |
|---|---|
| Pre-Pointed / Transition (1180) | Oakham Castle window 1180 (an isolated outlier — nothing falls between it and 1250) |
| First / Early English (1250–1300) | Lambeth chapel 1250, Brazen Nose doorway 1250, Apthorpe fireplace 1250, St John's Northampton from 1290, Wells chapel 1300 |
| Second / Middle Pointed (1320–1380) | Mayfield Palace, Guesten Hall and Carmelite gateway 1320; Battle Abbey gateway 1330; Penshurst and Norborough 1335; Great Malvern timber hall 1340; Spicer's Hall and Ely fireplace 1350; Lambeth Guard Room 1380 |
| Third / Perpendicular (1430–1500) | Higham Ferrers 1430, Ewelme 1440, St Cross and Canynge's 1450, Sudeley window and Colston's 1460, Butcher Row and Bede House Stamford and Malvern gateway 1490, Lyddington and Little Leigh conduit and foreign panelling 1500 |
| Late 16th c (1506–1582) | Bablake 1506, Ford's Hospital 1529, Lavenham 1530, Commandery 1535, Vyne panelling 1540, Haddon bay and Vyne chapel 1545, Holcombe Court 1550, Mayfield old house 1575, Prestbury 1580, Stamford bench ends 1582 |
| Post-medieval survival (1600–1637) | Ludlow 1600, Blundell's 1604, Guildford furniture 1625, Chiddingstone 1637 |

**Two structural facts.** Six entries are *ranges*, not points, and the range is an explicit statement that the fabric is multi-period — St John's Northampton 1290–1520, St Mary's Chichester 1300–1680, Cobham 1362–1598, Stirling 1450–1540, ironwork 1450–1700, Scottish ironwork "16th and 17th Centuries." Do not collapse them to a midpoint; that accretion case is exactly what a generator should be able to produce. And the ironwork being dated as a 250-year *band* says the authors regarded domestic ironwork profiles as period-insensitive, so one ironwork parts library serves the whole medieval range.

**The Scottish ladder is deliberately later**, and the authors say so: Scotland has "comparatively few remaining anterior to the 15th century" and the phases "were later in Scotland in point of date than in England." A Scottish preset needs a date offset of roughly 50–100 years *plus* a different form vocabulary (crow-stepped rather than straight gables, no four-centred arch, circular/segmental/semi-octagonal heads, barrel vault with applied ribs faking groining, turnpike stair). The dormer sub-run — Stirling 1550, Maybole 1620, Newark 1620, Hagg's 1685 — is a 135-year evolution sequence and all four are drawn on **one comparative sheet** at `dollman_v2` leaf 130.

Four corrections were applied here and the refuted claims are dropped:

- **Ordering.** The list is *not* strictly ascending within each class. Ascending holds within each class **or each named sub-run, the count restarting at each sub-type**. The Scottish WINDOWS class runs 1430, 1597, 1620, 1550, 1620, 1620, 1685, 1502 — the dormer sub-run restarts it and the Vennel loophole 1502 sits last, after 1685. The four ungrouped Scottish items below the short rule run 1600, 1450, 1597, i.e. not date-ordered at all.
- **The latest date in the work is 1685** (the Hagg's Castle dormer), not 1671. The Old House Elgin at 1671 is only the latest single-point date given to a whole building in the Scottish mansions class; Chichester is ranged to 1680 and the ironwork band runs to 1700.
- Of the two early Scottish fireplaces, **only Dirleton 1320** is earlier than the earliest dated Scottish mansion (Dunfermline 1340). Craigmillar 1427 is 87 years *later*, and the "detail vocabulary documented earlier than the envelope vocabulary" argument now rests on one example.
- The English span does **not** run 1180–1637 in an unbroken sequence at roughly 50-year intervals. The dense run starts at 1250 with 1180 an isolated outlier, and the English span actually reaches 1680/1700 via the Chichester range and the ironwork band.

**The promised analytical index does not exist.** The promise originates in volume 1's preface (`dollman_v1` leaf 11, August 1861), verbatim with long-s normalised: *"It is also intended that a resume of the subject of Ancient Domestic Architecture shall form a portion of the literary matter, embracing many curious and interesting particulars; and that a carefully arranged chronological and analytical index of all the subjects shall be introduced therein"*. Volume 2's preface (leaf 9, November 1863) restates it narrowed to the chronological half only. There are four `chronolog` hits in the two volumes — v1 leaf 11, v1 leaf 341 (incidental, Lambeth portraits "chronologically arranged"), v2 leaf 9, v2 leaf 26 — and the word `analytic` occurs **nowhere else in either volume**. The chronological half was delivered; the analytical half was never printed.

Two hard traps: the list has **no plate numbers and no leaf numbers**, so it cannot be used to locate a drawing; and its columns OCR as **separate blocks**, all names then all dates, so any name-to-date pairing derived from OCR is scrambled and worthless. Every pairing above was read off the images, where the leader dots make it unambiguous.

### The engraved volume plate number: a one-crop way to identify any volume 1 plate

**New, VERIFIED, and cheap to exploit.** Volume 1's plates carry a **continuous volume plate number** engraved in the outer top corner, independent of the per-subject `Pl. n`. Confirmed by opening and cropping: leaf 344 = `74.` (Lambeth Pl. 1), leaf 362 = `78` (Stirling Pl. 1), leaf 374 = `81.` (Stirling Pl. 4, the last plate in the volume). Leaf 18 = plate 1 (Chiddingstone Pl. 1), leaf 160 = `32. 4` (Guesten Hall Pl. 4), leaf 164 = `33 Pl.1`, leaf 168 = `34 Pl.2`, leaf 172 = `35 Pl.1`, leaf 184 = `38 Pl.1`, leaf 188 = `39 Pl.2`.

This matters because the number is **derivable in advance** from the cumulative plate counts on leaf 13. Running the leaf-13 list forward gives Chiddingstone 1–2, Oakham 3, Lyddington/Apthorpe 4, St John's 5–8, Dunfermline 9–16, St Cross 17–24, Great Malvern 25–27, Sudeley 28, Guesten Hall 29–32, ironwork 33, keys 34, Commandery 35–37, Mayfield house 38–39, Mayfield Palace 40–43, Linlithgow 44–53 … Lambeth 74–77, Stirling 78–81. Every one of those predictions that has been checked against an engraved number matches. So: predict the number from the list, crop the outer top corner, confirm. That is a far better identification method than anything OCR-based, and leaf 374 reading `81.` confirms the printed total of 81 **independently of the list**.

### Penshurst: the type-specimen, with its plan re-oriented

The authors nominate it themselves (v2 leaf 16): *"One of the best types of a mansion of the 14th century in its plan and general arrangements, is the beautiful example at Penshurst, in Kent, illustrated in our pages."* All ten leaf numbers are **VERIFIED** by opening each image and reading the engraved header — 52, 56, 60, 64, 68, 72, 76, 80, 84, 88 = engraved Pl.1–Pl.7, Pl.12 (= the text's Plate 8), Pl.9, Pl.10 — and the run is complete, leaf 92 being the Ludlow plate. The printed figures came from the estate's own architect, George Devey, so they are survey data.

**Plate 1's plan was being read upside down, and that is now fixed.** No north point is printed. The plan is drawn with **north at the bottom of the sheet**, 180° from the general plan on leaf 80 which *does* carry a north arrow — so **left = east, right = west**. At the bottom (north) of the screens passage is the **groined entrance porch**: a square compartment with dotted groin ribs, two canted buttresses facing the "Court", and a **spiral newel in its inner angle**. Walking in from the porch the three service doorways run **Buttary, Passage to Kitchen, Pantry**, and that order is what fixes the orientation, matching leaf 46 verbatim: *"the firft of which opened into the buttery; the fecond led to the kitchen … and the third doorway opened into the pantry."* At the far (south, top-of-sheet) end are the annotated "Step Step" and a **second spiral newel** to the minstrels' gallery. "Modern Buildings" lie to the **east**. The vaulted range beyond the hall is **four quadripartite compartments, two bays by two, on one central column** drawn as a solid dot with wall responds at each side's mid-point — not two bays.

The elevation is fixed too: the **porch bay is not gabled**. It carries the same embattled parapet and continuous carved-head corbel table as the hall wall, and a **battlemented stair turret** rises through the roof slope beside it. There is **one** heavy stepped buttress, at the porch junction; the member between the two windows is a plain shallow pilaster-buttress with a single moulded offset over a plinth, and the segmental relieving arches spring from it. "Saccharissa's Room" is a name from the **text** at leaf 46, not lettering on the plate. On the leaf-80 general plan the solid-black 14th-century poche covers the hall, the porch, the service rooms **and** the vaulted range G.

**Dimension corrections that would have produced wrong geometry:**

- **32 ft 0 in on Plate 2 is not floor-to-ridge.** Traced at magnification: upper arrowhead on the dashed line off the ridge, lower arrowhead on the dashed line at the top of the figure-corbel's moulded capital. It is **ridge to springing / wall-plate level**, so springing sits 16 ft 0 in above the floor (48 ft − 32 ft). Both the 32 ft 0 in and the 38 ft 9 in are *broken* dimensions running off the sheet.
- **64 ft 0 in includes the screens passage.** Its left arrowhead sits on the inner face of the service-range wall, about 8 ft west of the screen, so the hall *body* screen-to-dais-wall is roughly 55–56 ft — a proportion off the plan's own 0–50 FEET bar, not a printed figure. The dimension line is struck **below** the hearth, not through it; the hearth octagon is separately and correctly at the hall's centre.
- **2 ft 5 7/8 in is the clear width of one light**, between the *faces* of mullions B and C. Mullion centres are not printed; they work out at about 2 ft 11 3/4 in. The checkable identity is 4 × 2 ft 5 7/8 in clear + 3 × 5 7/8 in mullions = 11 ft 5 1/8 in, inside the printed 12 ft 3 in overall. **The old "4 bays × 2 ft 5 7/8 in = 9 ft 11 1/2 in" is dropped** — a generator spacing mullions at 2 ft 5 7/8 in centres compresses the window by about 1 ft 6 in.
- **The fire dog's height and length were swapped.** The standard is **3 ft 8 1/2 in** high, floor to the top of the moulded cap (a vertical dimension, confirmed against the plate's own 296 px/ft bar, which measures the standard at 3.60 ft). 3 ft 11 3/4 in and 5 ft 2 in are the two *horizontal* figures, both broken lines with a single left arrowhead — 3 ft 11 3/4 in struck at floor level, 5 ft 2 in at cap level — and because the right-hand terminus is not drawn, do not assert what they span. **5 ft 2 in is not the andiron's height**; that reading gives an andiron ~40 % too tall.
- 12 ft 3 in is simply the overall window width, struck symmetrically about the marked Centre Line (878 px left, 902 px right); the "includes the splayed internal reveal" caveat is dropped.
- **Scale trap:** on Plate 10 the hearth plan is drawn at roughly a *quarter* of the plate's stated detail scale (7 ft 7 in spans 543 px where the bar gives 296 px/ft). Only the printed 7 ft 7 in is usable; nothing may be scaled from that plan.

Two traps for the next reader. **The volume 2 OCR array is not aligned with the leaf/image index over leaves 52–98** — OCR page 56 carries the Plate 1 caption, OCR page 76 the Plate 3 caption — so identify plates from images only. And **the book's own list of illustrations (leaf 47) disagrees with the engraved headers for plates 2 and 3**: the list and every text cross-reference call the N/S windows "Plate 2" and the roof "Plate 3", while the engravings are Pl.2 = ROOF and Pl.3 = WINDOWS. Anyone chasing "Plate 2" from the text lands on the roof plate.

### Linlithgow: two figured dimensions that had been missed, and a plan re-labelled

`dollman_v1` leaf 214 (**VERIFIED** at 2664 × 3490, righted with `sips -r 90`) carries **two figured dimensions that the first reading described as absent**, and one of them is exactly what the asset brief asks for:

- **166 ft 2 in** — a running dimension along the ground line of the south elevation, the east–west length of the pile, of which the text's "166 feet" is a rounding.
- **66 ft 0 in** — an arrowheaded vertical dimension from the ground line to the parapet of the south-west tower. **This is the only figured height for Linlithgow anywhere in the work.**

The record's statement that the plate's only metric content is a 100 FEET scale bar is refuted.

Plan labels corrected: **King's Apart at the north-WEST and Kitchen at the north-EAST** (the record had them swapped, and the plate agrees with the text, which puts James III's bed and dressing rooms at the north-west angle and the upper kitchen north of the Parliament Hall). Five courtyard stair turrets is the right count, but they are NW, **north face centre** (drawn inside an octagonal bay — the text's *"in the centre has an octagonal stair-turret"* on the 1619–20 north range), NE, SW, SE; the fifth is not on the south face. The three outward round towers are on the **east** side, drawn clear of the main rectangle and dash-linked to the east front. The "Plan on Line X.Y" is the **bottom register spanning the whole sheet**, not a drawing at the right.

### Reading traps for the next pass

1. **The landscape folio plates are rotated 90° in the scan.** `sips -r 90` rights them (`-r -90` leaves them upside down). Consequence: an arrow that looks horizontal in the raw scan is a **vertical** dimension in reality. Native scan width is 2664 px for volume 1 and 2612 px for volume 2 — requesting more returns the native size.
2. **The per-leaf OCR array is offset from the images**, by about four leaves through volume 1's plate sections and demonstrably across volume 2 leaves 52–98. Identify every plate from the image and its engraved header, never from an OCR index.
3. **Non-zero-but-tiny OCR does not mean "plate leaf."** It is often letterpress bleeding forward from the preceding description leaves. Use the 400-px thumbnail file size, then the engraved corner number.
4. **Do not take a plate count or a date from this book's OCR.** Counts come out as `j`, `r`, `x`, `!`; the chronological list's columns OCR as separate blocks so every name-to-date pairing is scrambled.
5. **The list of illustrations and the engraved plate headers can disagree.** At Penshurst they transpose plates 2 and 3, and leaf 80's engraved header reads `Vol.2. Pl.12` for what the text calls Plate 8.
6. **1860s long-s typography.** Read `f` as `s` where it makes sense (`houfe` = house, `moft` = most, `defign` = design) and say so rather than quoting the corruption as if it were the text. Numerals suffer too: `166` appears as `1 66`, `106` as `1 06`.
7. **Check the drawing's own scale against the sheet's legend.** Penshurst's hearth plan is at about a quarter of its plate's stated detail scale, and Haddon's legend was misread by a factor of two from a low-resolution render.
8. **Distinguish "restored" from surveyed.** Shrewsbury's elevation is labelled `(RESTORED)`; Lavenham's north bays are the authors' inference from the surviving west one; Higham Ferrers' internal partitions had been "entirely removed" before the plate was drawn; the Little Leigh conduit's pinnacles are "fhewn on the Plate as reftored"; the Penshurst screen's battlements are lettered "Modern Battlements"; Chiddingstone's porch columns, doorway and gable window and Ludlow's finials and doors are all stated modern.
9. **The three "Old Oak Panelling" plates are catalogued by the authors as FOREIGN.** Do not ship them as generic English wainscot. The English example is the Vyne panelling.
