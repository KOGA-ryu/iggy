# Algebra: rational equations

Read ../BRIEF.md, ../assignments.json and source precalc_rational in ../SOURCES.json.
Only write your assigned six source files/authoring folder and
build/production/wave04/algebra/. This brief and the registry are coordinator-owned.
Package prod04_algebra_rational_equations version 1, reading suffix _r,
questions _q01 through _q12. Bind to topic_0004.

Use Section 3.7 Exercises 5-18 as seeds. They originally ask about rational
functions; turn the given functions into explicit equations f(x)=k, or related
rational equations, recording this change. At least six distinct exercises
must be represented. Prefer finite small integer coefficients, original
denominators factored into real linear factors and cleared numerators of
degree at most two with exact rational roots. Define the bounds before cases.

q01-q04 introduce one denominator and full solves; q05-q08 use multiple factors,
negative/fractional roots and cancellation; q09-q12 mix methods and exceptional
results. Include at least two cases rejecting a denominator-zero candidate,
one empty solution set and one identity on a restricted original domain.
Select a method in at least two mixed questions and complete it. Do not label
the identity case as all real numbers when the original denominator excludes some.

Define rational expression, denominator, domain, LCD, equivalence on that
domain, candidate and solution. Explain why multiplying by an LCD is reversible
only where it is nonzero. Original restrictions come before simplification.
Show all required terms multiplied by the LCD, solve the resulting equation,
filter candidates and substitute into the ORIGINAL equation. The identity
case proves the identity for every allowed input, rather than checking samples.

Check exact polynomial/rational arithmetic, all restrictions, denominators,
goal forms and complete solution sets. Include false cancellation, omitted
LCD term, extraneous roots, wrong domain and reordered-set/equivalent-fraction
probes. Scope is real equations; no inequalities, graphing or new assets.

Credit Lippman and Rasmussen, and Chapter 3's permitted remix of Stitz and
Zeager. Use the main text CC BY-SA 4.0; exclude the NC solutions manual and
UW-marked problems. Write explanations independently from the source seeds.
