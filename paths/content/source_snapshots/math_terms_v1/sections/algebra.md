## Algebraic Structures

### Set Foundations

#### Term: Algebra
**Algebra** is the branch of mathematics that studies symbols and the rules for manipulating them.

#### Term: Set
A **set** is a collection of distinct objects.

#### Term: Subset
\(A\subseteq B\) means every element of \(A\) also belongs to \(B\).

### Group Theory

#### Term: Group
A **group** is \((G,\ast)\) with closure, associativity, identity, and inverses.

#### Term: Cyclic Group
A group is **cyclic** if generated by a single element: \(G=\langle g\rangle\).

#### Term: Homomorphism
A map \(\varphi:G\to H\) is a **group homomorphism** if \(\varphi(x\ast y)=\varphi(x)\star\varphi(y)\).

#### Term: Subgroup
A **subgroup** \(H\subseteq G\) is a group under the inherited operation.

### Ring Theory

#### Term: Ring
A **ring** has two operations, with addition forming an abelian group and multiplication distributing over addition.

#### Term: Commutative Ring
A ring is **commutative** if multiplication is commutative: \(ab=ba\).

### Field Theory

#### Term: Field
A **field** is a commutative ring where every nonzero element has a multiplicative inverse.

#### Term: Characteristic of a Field
The **characteristic** is the smallest positive \(n\) such that \(n\cdot 1=0\), or \(0\) if no such \(n\) exists.

## Number Systems and Divisibility

### Number Sets

#### Term: Natural Numbers
\(\mathbb N\) typically denotes nonnegative integers or positive integers depending on convention.

#### Term: Integers
\(\mathbb Z\) is the set of whole numbers including negatives.

#### Term: Integral Domain
A commutative ring with identity and no zero divisors.

#### Term: Rationals, Reals, Complexes
\(\mathbb Q\subset\mathbb R\subset\mathbb C\).

### Divisibility and GCD

#### Term: Divides
\(a\mid b\) means there exists \(k\in\mathbb Z\) such that \(b=ka\).

#### Term: Greatest Common Divisor
\(\gcd(a,b)\) is the largest positive integer dividing both \(a\) and \(b\).

#### Term: Coprime
Integers \(a,b\) are **coprime** if \(\gcd(a,b)=1\).

### Congruence and Residue Classes

#### Term: Congruence
\(a\equiv b\pmod n\iff n\mid(a-b)\).

#### Term: Modulo Class
A **congruence class** modulo \(n\) contains numbers with the same remainder mod \(n\).

#### Term: Modular Inverse
For \(a\), the modular inverse \(a^{-1}\) (mod \(n\)) satisfies \(aa^{-1}\equiv1\pmod n\).

## Equations and Relations

### Equation Forms

#### Term: Equation
An **equation** is a statement \(LHS=RHS\).

#### Term: Identity
An identity \(f(x)=g(x)\) holds for all values in domain, not just one solution.

#### Term: Polynomial Equation
Equation where terms are polynomials: \(p(x)=0\).

### Functional Viewpoint

#### Term: Function
A **function** \(f:X\to Y\) maps each \(x\in X\) to one image in \(Y\).

#### Term: Injection
A function \(f\) is injective if \(f(a)=f(b)\Rightarrow a=b\).

#### Term: Surjection
A function \(f\) is surjective if every element of codomain has a preimage.

#### Term: Bijection
A function is **bijective** if it is injective and surjective.

### Algebraic Relations

#### Term: Isomorphism
An isomorphism is a bijective structure-preserving map.

#### Term: Equivalence Relation
Relation \(\sim\) is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive.

#### Term: Equivalence Class
For \(a\in S\), \([a]=\{x\in S\mid x\sim a\}\).

## Polynomials and Rational Expressions

### Polynomial Form

#### Term: Polynomial
A polynomial in \(x\): \(\sum_{k=0}^n a_kx^k\), with \(a_n\neq 0\).

#### Term: Leading Coefficient
In \(a_nx^n+\cdots+a_0\), coefficient \(a_n\) is the leading coefficient.

#### Term: Constant Term
The term with exponent \(0\): \(a_0\).

### Degree and Coefficients

#### Term: Degree
Degree is the largest exponent with nonzero coefficient.

#### Term: Multiplicity of a Root
The **multiplicity** of root \(r\) is the largest \(m\) such that \((x-r)^m\) divides the polynomial.

### Factorization Strategies

#### Term: Factorization
A rewrite into a product of simpler expressions.

#### Term: Quadratic Formula
Roots of \(ax^2+bx+c=0\): \(\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}\).

#### Term: Difference of Squares
\(a^2-b^2=(a-b)(a+b)\).

### Rational Expressions

#### Term: Rational Expression
A quotient \(\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}\) with \(q(x)\neq 0\).

#### Term: Rational Function
A function that is a ratio of two polynomials.

#### Term: Domain Restriction
A rational expression excludes zeros of denominator from its domain.

## Inequalities and Expressions

### Absolute Value

#### Term: Absolute Value
\(|x| = \begin{cases}x,&x\ge0\\-x,&x<0\end{cases}\).

#### Term: Product of Absolute Values
\(|ab|=|a||b|\).

### Ordering Facts

#### Term: Triangle Inequality
\(|a+b|\le |a|+|b|\).

#### Term: Reverse Triangle Inequality
\(||a|-|b||\le |a-b|\).

#### Term: AM-GM Inequality
For nonnegative reals \(a,b\): \(\sqrt{ab}\le \frac{a+b}{2}\).

## Symmetry and Group Actions

### Permutations

#### Term: Permutation
A bijection from a set to itself.

#### Term: Transposition
A permutation that swaps two elements and leaves others fixed.

### Group Actions

#### Term: Symmetric Group
\(S_n\) is the group of all permutations of \(\{1,\dots,n\}\).

#### Term: Orbit
For action \(G\curvearrowright X\), orbit of \(x\) is \(\{g\cdot x:g\in G\}\).

## Algebraic Methodology

### Proof Patterns

#### Term: Equivalence-Preserving Manipulation
Algebraic steps that maintain logical equivalence.

#### Term: Contradiction
Assume \(\neg P\), derive contradiction, conclude \(P\).

### Transformations and Composition

#### Term: Function Composition
\((g\circ f)(x)=g(f(x))\).

#### Term: Substitution
Replace a variable with an equal expression to simplify or reframe an equation.

## Formal Theorems

### Theorem (Bézout Identity)
For integers \(a,b\in\mathbb Z\), there exist \(x,y\in\mathbb Z\) such that
\[
ax+by=\gcd(a,b).
\]

### Theorem (Remainder Theorem)
For polynomial \(p(x)\), \((x-c)\mid p(x)\) iff \(p(c)=0\).

### Theorem (Lagrange's Theorem for Finite Groups)
If \(G\) is a finite group and \(H\le G\), then \(|H|\) divides \(|G|\).

### Theorem (Unique Factorization in \(\mathbb Z\))
Every integer \(n>1\) has a factorization
\[
 n=p_1^{\alpha_1}p_2^{\alpha_2}\cdots p_k^{\alpha_k},
\]
where \(p_i\) are prime and this expression is unique up to ordering.

## Worked Examples

### Example (Bézout via Euclidean Algorithm)
Compute \(\gcd(48,18)\):
\[
48=2\cdot 18+12,\quad 18=1\cdot 12+6,\quad 12=2\cdot 6.
\]
So \(\gcd(48,18)=6\).
From back substitution:
\[
6=18-12=18-(48-2\cdot 18)=3\cdot 18-1\cdot 48.
\]
Hence \(6=3\cdot 18+(-1)\cdot 48\).

### Example (Polynomial Factor via Root)
For \(p(x)=x^3-6x^2+11x-6\), evaluate
\[
p(1)=1-6+11-6=0.
\]
Thus \((x-1)\mid p(x)\) and
\[
 p(x)=(x-1)(x^2-5x+6)=(x-1)(x-2)(x-3).
\]

### Example (Subgroup in Cyclic Group)
In \(\mathbb Z_8\), generated element \(1\) gives
\(\langle 1\rangle=\{0,1,2,3,4,5,6,7\}=\mathbb Z_8\).
So \(\mathbb Z_8\) is cyclic; any subgroup order divides 8 (1,2,4,8).

## Worked Examples (Q to A Mini-Proofs)

### Q1. Find \(\gcd(48,18)\) and a Bézout representation.

**Answer.** Use the Euclidean algorithm:
\[
48 = 2\cdot18 + 12,
\]
\[
18 = 1\cdot12 + 6,
\]
\[
12 = 2\cdot6 + 0.
\]
So \(\gcd(48,18)=6\).
Back-substitute to write \(6\) as a combination of \(48\) and \(18\):
\[
6=18-1\cdot12
=18-(48-2\cdot18)=3\cdot18-1\cdot48.
\]
Hence \(6=3\cdot18+(-1)\cdot48\), a valid Bézout form.

### Q2. Factor \(p(x)=x^3-6x^2+11x-6\) into linear factors.

**Answer.** Evaluate at \(x=1\):
\[
p(1)=1-6+11-6=0.
\]
So \((x-1)\mid p(x)\). Perform synthetic division (or polynomial division):
\[
p(x)=(x-1)(x^2-5x+6).
\]
Then
\[
x^2-5x+6=(x-2)(x-3).
\]
Therefore
\[
p(x)=(x-1)(x-2)(x-3).
\]

### Q3. Show that in \(\mathbb Z_8\), the element \([2]\) generates a subgroup of size 4.

**Answer.** Compute repeated multiples under addition modulo 8:
\[
[0],[2],[4],[6],[0],\dots
\]
There are 4 distinct elements before returning to \([0]\): \([0],[2],[4],[6]\).
Hence \(\langle [2]\rangle=\{[0],[2],[4],[6]\}\), so this subgroup has order 4. This also matches Lagrange’s theorem: 4 divides \(8\).

### Q4. Prove \((x-2)\mid x^3-6x^2+11x-6\).

**Answer.** By the remainder theorem, \((x-c)\mid p(x)\) iff \(p(c)=0\).
Set \(c=2\):
\[
p(2)=8-24+22-6=0.
\]
Thus \((x-2)\) divides the polynomial.

## Additional Mini-Proof Examples (Round 3)

### Q5. Prove that the quotient of two nonzero rational numbers is rational.

**Answer.** Let \(a,b,c,d\in\mathbb Z\) with \(a/b\neq 0\), \(c/d\neq 0\), and \(b,d\neq 0\).
Then
\[
\frac{a/b}{c/d}=\frac{a}{b}\cdot\frac{d}{c}=\frac{ad}{bc}.
\]
Since \(ad\in\mathbb Z\) and \(bc\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}\), the result is rational by definition.

### Q6. Show that \(\sqrt{2}\) is irrational.

**Answer.** Assume for contradiction \(\sqrt2\) is rational. Then \(\sqrt2=\frac{p}{q}\) with \(\gcd(p,q)=1\), \(q>0\).
Squaring gives:
\[
2=\frac{p^2}{q^2},\quad p^2=2q^2.
\]
So \(p^2\) is even, hence \(p\) even, so \(p=2k\).
Then
\[
(2k)^2=2q^2\Rightarrow 2k^2=q^2,
\]
so \(q^2\) even and \(q\) even. Contradiction because \(p,q\) cannot both be even while coprime. Therefore \(\sqrt2\) irrational.

### Q7. Find the value of \(|-7|+|3|-|-4|\).

**Answer.** By definition, \(|-7|=7\), \(|3|=3\), \(|-4|=4\).
Thus
\[
|-7|+|3|-|-4| = 7+3-4=6.
\]

### Q8. Verify \((\mathbb Z,+)\) is a group while \((\mathbb N,+)\) is not.

**Answer.** 
- In \((\mathbb Z,+)\), associativity and identity \(0\) hold; every integer \(n\) has inverse \(-n\in\mathbb Z\).
- In \((\mathbb N,+)\) with \(\mathbb N\) nonnegative, additivity and associativity hold but negatives are absent. For example, inverse of \(1\) would require \(-1\notin\mathbb N\).
Hence \((\mathbb Z,+)\) is a group and \((\mathbb N,+)\) is not.

## Formal Theorems (Round 4: Standard Statement Pack)

### Theorem (Euclidean Division Algorithm for Integers)
For integers \(a\) and positive integer \(b\), there exist unique integers \(q,r\) such that
\[
a = bq + r,\quad 0 \le r < b.
\]

### Theorem (Rational Root Theorem)
If \(p(x)=a_nx^n+\cdots+a_0\in\mathbb Z[x]\) with \(a_n\neq 0\), and \(\frac{p}{q}\in\mathbb Q\) (in lowest terms) is a root of \(p\), then
\[p\mid a_0 \text{ and } q\mid a_n.\]

### Theorem (Chinese Remainder Theorem for Two Moduli)
If \(m,n\in\mathbb N\) satisfy \(\gcd(m,n)=1\), then for any integers \(a,b\), the system
\[
x\equiv a\pmod m,\quad x\equiv b\pmod n
\]
has a solution, and the solution is unique modulo \(mn\).

### Theorem (Lagrange Interpolation)
Given distinct nodes \((x_i,y_i)\in\mathbb F^2\), \(i=0,\dots,n\), with \(x_i\neq x_j\) for \(i\neq j\), there exists a unique polynomial \(p\in\mathbb F[x]\) with \(\deg p\le n\) such that \(p(x_i)=y_i\) for all \(i\).

### Theorem (Unique Factorization of Polynomials over a Field)
Every nonzero polynomial \(p\in F[x]\) over a field \(F\) factors uniquely (up to ordering and multiplication by units) into irreducible polynomials.

## Round 4 Theorem Proof Sketches

### Q1. Euclidean Division (Proof idea)
Let \(a\in\mathbb Z\), \(b\in\mathbb N\). Define \(S=\{a-bq\mid q\in\mathbb Z,\ a-bq\ge 0\}\). Set \(S\) is nonempty and bounded below by 0, so by the Well-Ordering Principle it has minimum \(r\).
Then write \(a=bq+r\). If \(r\ge b\), then \(r-b\ge 0\) and \(r-b\in S\), contradicting minimality. So \(0\le r<b\).
If there were two such pairs \((q,r)\neq(q',r')\), then \(b(q-q')=r'-r\) with \(|r'-r|<b\) and \(b\mid(r'-r)\), forcing \(r=r'\), so \(q=q'\).

### Q2. Rational Root Theorem (Proof idea)
Suppose \(p(x)=a_nx^n+\cdots+a_0\) and \(\frac{p}{q}\) is a reduced root (\(\gcd(p,q)=1\)). Then
\[
0=p\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)=a_n\frac{p^n}{q^n}+\cdots+a_0
\]
Multiply by \(q^n\):
\[
 a_np^n + a_{n-1}p^{n-1}q+\cdots+a_1pq^{n-1}+a_0q^n=0.
\]
Rearrange
\[
 a_0q^n = -p(a_np^{n-1}+\cdots+a_1q^{n-1}).
\]
So \(p\mid a_0q^n\). Since \(\gcd(p,q)=1\), \(\gcd(p, q^n)=1\), thus \(p\mid a_0\).
Similarly
\[
 a_np^n = -q(a_{n-1}p^{n-1}q^{n-1}+\cdots+a_0q^{n-1}),
\]
so \(q\mid a_np^n\), hence \(q\mid a_n\).

### Q3. Chinese Remainder (Two-Moduli Form) (Proof idea)
Set \(x = a + m t\). Congruence \(x\equiv b\pmod n\) becomes
\[
a+mt\equiv b\pmod n \iff m t\equiv b-a\pmod n.
\]
Since \(\gcd(m,n)=1\), multiply both sides by inverse of \(m\) mod \(n\) to solve for \(t\) modulo \(n\), so solution exists and is unique mod \(n\). Then \(x=a+mt\) is unique mod \(mn\).

### Q4. Lagrange Interpolation (Existence and uniqueness)
Define basis polynomials
\[
L_i(x)=\prod_{j\ne i}\frac{x-x_j}{x_i-x_j},\quad i=0,\dots,n.
\]
Each \(L_i(x_j)=\delta_{ij}\). Then
\[
p(x)=\sum_{i=0}^n y_iL_i(x)
\]
gives \(p(x_i)=y_i\). Each \(L_i\) has degree \(n\), so \(\deg p\le n\).
For uniqueness: if \(q\) another polynomial of degree \(\le n\) satisfies same data, then \(r=p-q\) has at least \(n+1\) roots \(x_0,\dots,x_n\), so\(r\equiv 0\), thus \(p=q\).

### Q5. Factorization in \(F[x]\) (Proof sketch)
Because \(F[x]\) is a Euclidean domain, it is a unique factorization domain (UFD). Every nonzero nonunit in a UFD factors into irreducibles and factorization is unique up to order and units. Therefore every polynomial admits unique factorization as claimed.

## Round 6 Supplemental Terms

### Advanced Ring-Theoretic Terms

#### Term: Noetherian Ring

a ring in which every ascending chain of ideals stabilizes; equivalently every ideal is finitely generated.

#### Term: Principal Ideal Domain

a domain in which every ideal is principal, i.e., of the form \((a)\) for some \(a\).

#### Term: Euclidean Domain

a domain equipped with a norm \(N:R\setminus\{0\}\to \mathbb N\) such that division with remainder holds.

#### Term: Irreducible Element

a nonzero nonunit \(a\in R\) where \(a=bc\) implies either \(b\) or \(c\) is a unit.

#### Term: Maximal Ideal

a proper ideal \(M\subsetneq R\) such that there is no ideal strictly between \(M\) and \(R\).

## Round 7 Supplemental Terms

### Algebraic and Module Extensions

#### Term: Dedekind Domain

A Noetherian, integrally closed integral domain in which every nonzero prime ideal is maximal.

#### Term: Unique Factorization Domain

An integral domain where each nonzero nonunit factors uniquely (up to order and units) into irreducibles.

#### Term: Fraction Field

For an integral domain \(R\), its **fraction field** \(\mathrm{Frac}(R)\) is the smallest field containing \(R\), with elements \(a/b\).

#### Term: Localization

Given a multiplicative set \(S\subseteq R\), the localization \(S^{-1}R\) is the ring of formal fractions \(a/s\) with \(a\in R,\;s\in S\), where elements of \(S\) become units.

#### Term: Radical Ideal

The radical of \(I\subseteq R\) is
\[
\sqrt I=\{x\in R\mid x^n\in I\text{ for some }n\ge1\}.
\]

#### Term: Pushout
Given 


$f:A\to B,\,g:A\to C$, the **pushout** is the universal object gluing 


$B$ and 


$C$ 


along 


$A$.


## Round 11 Supplemental Terms

### Galois and Homological Structures

#### Term: Field Extension
A **field extension** is an inclusion of fields 


$\mathbb F\subseteq\mathbb E$, where 


$\mathbb E$ is a field containing a copy of 


$\mathbb F$.

#### Term: Degree of an Extension
For 


$\mathbb E/\mathbb F$, the **degree** is 


$[\mathbb E:\mathbb F]=\dim_{\mathbb F}\mathbb E$.

#### Term: Splitting Field
A **splitting field** of a polynomial 


$p(x)$ over 


$\mathbb F$ is the smallest field extension where 


$p\) splits into linear factors.

#### Term: Galois Group
For 


$\mathbb E/\mathbb F$ Galois, its **Galois group** is


\[\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb E/\mathbb F)=\{\sigma:\mathbb E\to\mathbb E\mid \sigma|_{\mathbb F}=\operatorname{id}\},\]


with composition as group operation.

#### Term: Algebraic Element
An element 


$\alpha\in\mathbb E$ is **algebraic** over 


$\mathbb F$ if it is a root of some nonzero polynomial in 


$\mathbb F[x]$.


## Round 12 Supplemental Terms

### Category-Theoretic Extensions

#### Term: Category
A **category** consists of objects and morphisms where morphisms compose associatively and each object has an identity morphism.

#### Term: Functor
A **functor** sends objects and morphisms from one category to another, preserving identities and composition.

#### Term: Natural Transformation
A **natural transformation** is a family of morphisms between two functors that commutes with all morphisms in the source category.

#### Term: Adjunction
An **adjunction** between categories is data \((F\dashv G)\) with a natural bijection
\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal D}(F(A),B)\cong \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal C}(A,G(B)).
\]

#### Term: Yoneda Lemma
For object \(X\) in locally small \(\mathcal C\), evaluation at \(X\) identifies
\[
\operatorname{Nat}(\operatorname{Hom}(X,-),F)\cong F(X)
\]
naturally in every functor \(F\).

## Round 13 Supplemental Terms

### Algebraic Geometry Bridges

#### Term: Ideal
An **ideal** \(I\subset R\) of a commutative ring \(R\) is a subset closed under addition and under multiplication by elements of \(R\).

#### Term: Prime Ideal
An ideal \(\mathfrak p\subset R\) is **prime** if \(ab\in\mathfrak p\) implies \(a\in\mathfrak p\) or \(b\in\mathfrak p\).

#### Term: Nilradical
The **nilradical** of \(R\) is
\[
\sqrt{(0)}=\{a\in R:a^n=0\text{ for some }n\in\mathbb N\}.
\]

#### Term: Prime Spectrum
The **prime spectrum** \(\operatorname{Spec}R\) is the set of prime ideals of \(R\) with Zariski topology.

#### Term: Radical Ideal
For ideal \(I\), the **radical** is
\[
\sqrt I=\{a\in R: a^n\in I\text{ for some }n\in\mathbb N\}.
\]

## Round 14 Supplemental Terms

### Advanced Homological Algebra

#### Term: Short Exact Sequence
A sequence

a \xrightarrow{f} b \xrightarrow{g} c\n is **exact** iff 
\[
\mathrm{im}(f)=\ker(g).
\]

#### Term: Split Exact Sequence
A short exact sequence is **split** if it is isomorphic to
\(0\to A\to A\oplus C\to C\to 0\), so the middle term decomposes as a direct sum.

#### Term: Derived Functor
For a functor \(F\), its derived functors \(R^iF\) measure failure of exactness, e.g.\(R^iF(X)=0\) for injective resolutions when \(F\) is exact.

#### Term: Projective Object
An object \(P\) is **projective** if \(\mathrm{Hom}(P,-)\) is exact; equivalently every epimorphism \(f:P\twoheadrightarrow X\) lifts through surjections.

#### Term: Yoneda Extension Group
\(
\mathrm{Ext}^1(A,B)\) classifies short exact sequences
\(0\to B\to E\to A\to 0\) up to equivalence.

## Round 15 Supplemental Terms

### Homological and Derived Structures

#### Term: Projective Resolution
A **projective resolution** of an object \(A\) is an exact sequence
\[
\cdots \to P_2 \to P_1 \to P_0 \to A \to 0
\]
where each \(P_i\) is projective.

#### Term: Tor Functor
For modules \(M,N\),\(\mathrm{Tor}_n^R(M,N)\) is the left-derived functor of tensor; it measures failure of exactness of \(\otimes\).

#### Term: Derived Category
The derived category \(D(A)\) is obtained from chain complexes by formally inverting quasi-isomorphisms.

#### Term: Flat Module
A module \(M\) is **flat** if tensoring with it preserves exact sequences.

#### Term: Ext in Degree One
\(\mathrm{Ext}^1_R(M,N)\) classifies equivalence classes of short exact sequences
\(0\to N\to E\to M\to 0\).

## Round 16 Supplemental Terms

### Spectral and Category Extensions

#### Term: Exact Functor
A functor \(F\) is **exact** if it preserves exact sequences; equivalently, exactness of \(0\to A\to B\to C\to 0\) implies exactness of \(0\to F(A)\to F(B)\to F(C)\to 0\).

#### Term: Noetherian Module
A module \(M\) is **Noetherian** if every ascending chain of submodules stabilizes.

#### Term: Artinian Module
A module \(M\) is **Artinian** if every descending chain of submodules stabilizes.

#### Term: Composition Series
A finite filtration
\[
0=M_0\subset M_1\subset\cdots\subset M_n=M
\]
with simple factors \(M_i/M_{i-1}\) is a composition series.

#### Term: Jordan--Hölder Multiset
In a finite-length module, the multiset of composition factors is uniquely determined up to isomorphism and order.

## Round 17 Supplemental Terms

### Adjunctions and Representability

#### Term: Adjunction
A pair \((F,G)\) is an **adjunction** \(F\dashv G\) if there is a natural bijection
\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal D}(F A,B)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal C}(A,G B)
\]
for objects \(A\in\mathcal C\), \(B\in\mathcal D\).

#### Term: Unit of Adjunction
An adjunction has natural maps \(\eta: \mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal C}\to G F\) and \(\varepsilon: F G\to \mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal D}\).

#### Term: Representable Functor
A functor \(F:\mathcal C\to\mathbf{Set}\) is representable if \(F\cong\operatorname{Hom}(A,-)\) for some \(A\in\mathcal C\).

#### Term: Yoneda Embedding
The assignment \(X\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}(X,-)\) defines a fully faithful functor
\(\mathcal C\to[\mathcal C,\mathbf{Set}]\).

#### Term: Torsion Pair
A pair \((\mathcal T,\mathcal F)\) with \(\mathrm{Hom}(T,F)=0\) for \(T\in\mathcal T,F\in\mathcal F\), closed under extension in an abelian context.

## Round 18 Supplemental Terms

### Topos-like Limits and Constructions

#### Term: Projective Limit
The **projective limit** of a system \((A_i, f_{ij})\) is
\[
\varprojlim_i A_i = \left\{(a_i)\in\prod_i A_i\;\big|\;f_{ij}(a_j)=a_i\text{ for }i\le j\right\}.
\]

#### Term: Inductive Limit
The **inductive limit** \(\varinjlim A_i\) is the universal object receiving compatible maps from all \(A_i\).

#### Term: Exactness of filtered colimits
A filtered colimit of short exact sequences is exact in modules (equivalently, filtered colimits are exact in \(\mathbf{Mod}_R\)).

#### Term: Derived Limit
\(\varprojlim^1 A_i\) denotes the first right-derived functor of \(\varprojlim\), measuring failure of right exactness.

#### Term: Compact Object
An object \(C\) is **compact** if\(\operatorname{Hom}(C,-)\) commutes with arbitrary filtered colimits.

## Round 19 Supplemental Terms

### Spectral Sequences and Filtrations

#### Term: Exact Couple
An **exact couple** is a diagram
\[D\xrightarrow{i}D\xrightarrow{j}E\xrightarrow{k}D\]
with exactness at each vertex in the repeating sequence
\[\cdots\to D\xrightarrow{i}D\xrightarrow{j}E\xrightarrow{k}D\xrightarrow{i}D\cdots\].

#### Term: Spectral Sequence
A **spectral sequence** is a collection \((E_r^{p,q},d_r)\) for \(r\ge2\), where each
\(d_r:E_r^{p,q}\to E_r^{p+r,q-r+1}\), and 
\(E_{r+1}^{p,q}\cong H(E_r^{p,q},d_r)\).

#### Term: Filtration
A **filtration** of object \(A\) is a chain
\(\cdots\subseteq F^{p+1}A\subseteq F^pA\subseteq\cdots\subseteq A\).

#### Term: Associated Graded Object
Given filtration \(F^\bullet A\), the associated graded object is
\(\mathrm{Gr}^pA=F^pA/F^{p+1}A\).

#### Term: Spectral Sequence Convergence
A spectral sequence \((E_r,d_r)\) **converges** to \(H\) if it stabilizes on pages
\(E_\infty\) and reproduces a filtered object with associated graded isomorphic to \(E_\infty\).

## Round 20 Supplemental Terms

### Derived Category Stability

#### Term: Triangulated Category
A **triangulated category** is an additive category with shift functor \([1]\) and distinguished triangles
\(X\to Y\to Z\to X[1]\), satisfying Verdier axioms.

#### Term: t-Structure
A **t-structure** on a triangulated category \(\mathcal D\) is a pair \((\mathcal D^{\le 0},\mathcal D^{\ge 0})\) such that
\(\mathcal D^{\le 0}\subseteq\mathcal D^{\le 1}\), \(\mathcal D^{\ge 0}\supseteq\mathcal D^{\ge 1}\), and every object fits into a triangle with truncations.

#### Term: Heart of a t-Structure
The **heart** is \(\mathcal A=\mathcal D^{\le 0}\cap\mathcal D^{\ge 0}\), which is an abelian category.

#### Term: Long Exact Cohomology Sequence
For a distinguished triangle \(X\to Y\to Z\to X[1]\), there is a long exact sequence
\(\cdots\to H^n(X)\to H^n(Y)\to H^n(Z)\to H^{n+1}(X)\to\cdots\).

#### Term: Compact Generation
A triangulated category is **compactly generated** if a set of compact objects detects zero objects via\(\mathrm{Hom}(C, -)=0\).

## Round 21 Supplemental Terms

### Higher Categorical Constructions

#### Term: Thick Subcategory
A full triangulated subcategory \(\mathcal T\subset\mathcal D\) is **thick** if closed under direct summands and distinguished triangles.

#### Term: Verdier Quotient
For triangulated \(\mathcal D\) and thick subcategory \(\mathcal T\), the Verdier quotient \(\mathcal D/\mathcal T\) formally inverts morphisms whose cone lies in \(\mathcal T\).

#### Term: Bousfield Localization
A **Bousfield localization** is an exact functor \(L:\mathcal D\to\mathcal D\) with natural map \(\eta: \mathrm{Id}\to L\), such that \(L\eta\) and \(\eta L\) are isomorphisms and \(L\eta_X\) is initial among maps to \(L\)-local objects.

#### Term: Brown Representability
A triangulated category satisfies **Brown representability** if every cohomological functor that converts coproducts to products is representable.

#### Term: Derived Equivalence
Two rings/category \(A,B\) are **derived equivalent** if
\(D(A)\simeq D(B)\), often through a tilting complex or Fourier--Mukai-style transform.

## Round 22 Supplemental Terms

### Higher Homological and Noncommutative Constructions

#### Term: Grothendieck Category
An abelian category \(\mathcal A\) is a **Grothendieck category** if it has exact filtered colimits, a generator, and arbitrary coproducts.

#### Term: Compact Object
An object \(C\) is **compact** if
\[
\mathrm{Hom}(C,\bigoplus_i X_i)\cong\bigoplus_i\mathrm{Hom}(C,X_i).
\]

#### Term: Tensor Category
A **tensor category** is a category \((\mathcal C,\otimes,\mathbf1)\) with a bifunctor \(\otimes\) and associativity/unit constraints.

#### Term: Quillen Adjunction
A pair \(L\dashv R\) is a **Quillen adjunction** if \(L\) preserves cofibrations and \(R\) preserves fibrations.

#### Term: Abelianization
For a group \(G\), the **abelianization** is \(G^{ab}=G/[G,G]\), the universal abelian quotient.

## Round 23 Supplemental Terms

### Homological Stability and Higher Algebra

#### Term: Monoidal Category
A **monoidal category** has a tensor product \(\otimes\), unit object \(\mathbf 1\), and coherent associativity constraints.

#### Term: Tannakian Category
For a field \(k\), a **Tannakian category** is a rigid symmetric monoidal \(k\)-linear category with a faithful exact fiber functor.

#### Term: Koszul Complex
Given \(R\)-sequence \(x_1,\dots,x_n\), the **Koszul complex** \(K_\bullet(x_1,\dots,x_n)\) computes algebraic dependence of the sequence.

#### Term: Ext Functor
For modules \(M,N\), \(\mathrm{Ext}^n_R(M,N)\) is the right-derived functor of \(\mathrm{Hom}_R(M,-)\), classifying \(n\)-fold extensions.

#### Term: Morita Equivalence
Rings \(A\) and \(B\) are **Morita equivalent** if \(A\)-Mod and \(B\)-Mod are equivalent categories.
