## Angle System

### Units and Radians

#### Term: Degree Measure
One full turn equals \(360^\circ\).

#### Term: Radian
One radian is arc length divided by radius; full turn is \(2\pi\) radians.

#### Term: Steradian
A **steradian** is a unit of solid angle in 3D; the full sphere subtends \(4\pi\) steradians.

### Angular Position

#### Term: Reference Angle
Smallest nonnegative angle from terminal side to x-axis.

#### Term: Coterminal Angles
Angles \(\theta\) and \(\theta+2k\pi\) have same terminal side for integer \(k\).

## Right Triangle Definitions

### Primary Ratios

#### Term: Sine
\(\sin\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\).

#### Term: Cosine
\(\cos\theta=\frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\).

#### Term: Tangent
\(\tan\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}\).

### Reciprocal Functions

#### Term: Secant
\(\sec\theta=\frac{1}{\cos\theta}\).

#### Term: Cosecant
\(\csc\theta=\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\).

#### Term: Cotangent
\(\cot\theta=\frac{1}{\tan\theta}=\frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}\).

## Unit Circle Framework

### Coordinate Representation

#### Term: Unit Circle
Set of points \((x,y)\) satisfying \(x^2+y^2=1\).

#### Term: Sine-Cosine Pair
For angle \(\theta\), coordinates are \((\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\).

### Reference Values

#### Term: Quadrant Sign Rule
In each quadrant, signs of \(\sin\theta\), \(\cos\theta\), \(\tan\theta\) are determined by \(x,y\) signs.

#### Term: Special Angles
Common exact values use \(\{30^\circ,45^\circ,60^\circ\}\).

## Identities

### Pythagorean Identities

#### Term: Pythagorean Identity
\(\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1\).

#### Term: Reciprocal Pythagorean Identity
\(1+\cot^2\theta=\csc^2\theta\).

### Angle Identities

#### Term: Angle Addition Formula
\(\sin(\alpha+\beta)=\sin\alpha\cos\beta+\cos\alpha\sin\beta\).

#### Term: Double-Angle Formula
\(\sin(2\theta)=2\sin\theta\cos\theta\).

#### Term: Product-to-Sum Identity
\(\sin\alpha\sin\beta=\frac12[\cos(\alpha-\beta)-\cos(\alpha+\beta)]\).

## Graphs and Periodic Behavior

### Periodicity

#### Term: Period
\(f(x+T)=f(x)\) for minimal \(T>0\).

#### Term: Phase Shift
\(\sin(x-\phi)\) shifts graph right by \(\phi\).

### Transform Characteristics

#### Term: Amplitude
For \(A\sin x\), amplitude is \(|A|\).

#### Term: Midline
Midline of \(A\sin x+B\) is the horizontal line \(y=B\).

## Inverse Trigonometry

### Inverse Functions

#### Term: Arcsine
\(\arcsin x\) gives \(\theta\) with \(\sin\theta=x\).

#### Term: Arccosine
\(\arccos x\) gives \(\theta\) with \(\cos\theta=x\).

### Principal Values

#### Term: Arctangent Principal Value
\(\arctan x\) takes values in \((-\pi/2,\pi/2)\).

#### Term: Atan2 Function
\(\operatorname{atan2}(y,x)\) gives angle accounting for quadrant of \((x,y)\).

## Applications

### Triangles

#### Term: Law of Sines
For triangle \(ABC\), \(\frac{a}{\sin A}=\frac{b}{\sin B}=\frac{c}{\sin C}\).

#### Term: Area by Sines
Triangle area: \(\frac12 ab\sin C\).

### Equations and Solving

#### Term: Basic Trigonometric Equation
\(\sin x = k\) has two principal reference-angle families before periodic extension.

#### Term: Harmonic Motion Equation
Standard form: \(x(t)=A\cos(\omega t+\phi)\).

## Formal Theorems

### Theorem (Pythagorean Identity)
For all real \(\theta\),
\[
\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1.
\]

### Theorem (Angle Sum Formula)
For all real \(\alpha,\beta\),
\[
\sin(\alpha+\beta)=\sin\alpha\cos\beta+\cos\alpha\sin\beta,
\]
\[
\cos(\alpha+\beta)=\cos\alpha\cos\beta-\sin\alpha\sin\beta.
\]

### Theorem (Law of Sines)
For triangle \(ABC\),
\[
\frac{a}{\sin A}=\frac{b}{\sin B}=\frac{c}{\sin C}=2R,
\]
where \(R\) is circumradius.

### Theorem (Periodicity of Sine and Cosine)
\(\sin(\theta+2\pi)=\sin\theta\), \(\cos(\theta+2\pi)=\cos\theta\).

## Worked Examples

### Example (Special Angle Evaluation)
\[
\sin 30^\circ=\frac12,\quad \cos 60^\circ=\frac12,\quad \tan 45^\circ=1.
\]

### Example (Use of Angle Addition)
\[
\sin 75^\circ=\sin(45^\circ+30^\circ)=\frac{\sqrt 2}{2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{2}+\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\cdot\frac12=\frac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4}.
\]

### Example (Solve a Trig Equation)
Solve \(\sin x=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\):
\[
x=\frac{\pi}{4}+2k\pi\quad\text{or}\quad x=\frac{3\pi}{4}+2k\pi,\quad k\in\mathbb Z.
\]

## Worked Examples (Q to A Mini-Proofs)

### Q1. Evaluate \(\sin 75^\circ\) exactly.

**Answer.** Use addition with \(75^\circ=45^\circ+30^\circ\):
\[
\sin75^\circ=\sin(45^\circ+30^\circ)
=\sin45^\circ\cos30^\circ+\cos45^\circ\sin30^\circ
\]
\[
=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{2}+\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\cdot\frac12
=\frac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4}.
\]

### Q2. Solve \(\sin x=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\) for real \(x\).

**Answer.** First principal solutions in \([0,2\pi)\) are \(x=\frac{\pi}{4}\) and \(x=\frac{3\pi}{4}\) in quadrant I and II.
Periodicity gives period \(2\pi\), so the full solution set is
\[
x=\frac{\pi}{4}+2k\pi\quad\text{or}\quad x=\frac{3\pi}{4}+2k\pi,\quad k\in\mathbb Z.
\]

### Q3. Prove the periodicity of sine: \(\sin(\theta+2\pi)=\sin\theta\).

**Answer.** On the unit circle, angles differing by \(2\pi\) have the same terminal point because one full revolution returns to the same point. Therefore both coordinates are equal, including the y-coordinate, so \(\sin(\theta+2\pi)=\sin\theta\). The same argument gives \(\cos(\theta+2\pi)=\cos\theta\).

### Q4. Verify \(\arcsin\left(\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\right)=\frac{\pi}{4}\).

**Answer.** Since by definition \(\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right)=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\), and \(\arcsin\) returns the unique value in \([-\pi/2,\pi/2]\) with the given sine, the answer follows.

## Additional Mini-Proof Examples (Round 3)

### Q5. Prove \(\tan\theta=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}\) when \(\cos\theta\neq 0\).

**Answer.** By definition in right-triangle interpretation, \(\sin\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\) and \(\cos\theta = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\).
When \(\cos\theta\neq 0\), adjacent \(\neq 0\). Then
\[
\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}=
\frac{\text{opp}/\text{hyp}}{\text{adj}/\text{hyp}}=
\frac{\text{opp}}{\text{adj}}=\tan\theta.
\]

### Q6. Derive \(\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1\) from unit circle coordinates.

**Answer.** A point on unit circle at angle \(\theta\) is \((\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\). It satisfies
\[
\cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta=1^2=1,
\]
because radius is 1.

### Q7. Solve \(\cos x =0\) on \([0,2\pi)\).

**Answer.** \(\cos x=0\) at angles whose terminal point has x-coordinate 0:
\[
x=\frac{\pi}{2},\;\frac{3\pi}{2}.
\]
These are the only solutions in the interval.

### Q8. Prove \(\sin(-\theta)=-\sin\theta\).

**Answer.** On the unit circle, angle \(-\theta\) reflects \((\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\) across the x-axis, which changes y-coordinate sign and leaves x-coordinate unchanged.
Hence \(\cos(-\theta)=\cos\theta\) (even) and \(\sin(-\theta)=-\sin\theta\) (odd).

## Formal Theorems (Round 4: Standard Statement Pack)

### Theorem (Law of Cosines)
For a triangle with side lengths \(a,b,c\) opposite corresponding angles \(A,B,C\):
\[
 c^2=a^2+b^2-2ab\cos C,
\]
and cyclic permutations hold.

### Theorem (Sine Law)
For a triangle \(ABC\) with inradius \(R\) (circumradius),
\[
\frac{a}{\sin A}=\frac{b}{\sin B}=\frac{c}{\sin C}=2R,
\]
where \(a,b,c\) are opposite sides.

### Theorem (Half-Angle Formula Set)
For \(\theta\in\mathbb R\),
\[
\sin^2\frac{\theta}{2}=\frac{1-\cos\theta}{2},\qquad
\cos^2\frac{\theta}{2}=\frac{1+\cos\theta}{2}.
\]

### Theorem (Inverse Sine Principal Value Existence and Uniqueness)
There is a unique function \(\arcsin:(-1,1)\to\left[-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}\right]\) such that
\[
\sin(\arcsin x)=x\quad(\forall x\in[-1,1]).
\]

### Theorem (Sum-to-Product)
For all real \(x,y\),
\[
\sin x+\sin y=2\sin\frac{x+y}{2}\cos\frac{x-y}{2},\qquad
\cos x+\cos y=2\cos\frac{x+y}{2}\cos\frac{x-y}{2}.
\]

## Round 4 Theorem Proof Sketches

### Q1. Law of Cosines (Proof idea)
In triangle with sides \(a,b,c\), drop altitude from vertex of angle \(C\) to side \(c\): split into right triangle pieces of lengths \(b\cos C\) and \(b\sin C\). Then remaining segment on side \(c\) is \(a-b\cos C\).
By Pythagoras:
\[
 c^2 = (a-b\cos C)^2 + (b\sin C)^2 = a^2 + b^2 -2ab\cos C.
\]
This is exactly the law.

### Q2. Sine Law (Proof idea)
Use triangle area in two ways:
\[
[\triangle ABC]=\frac12 ab\sin C = \frac12 bc\sin A=\frac12 ca\sin B.
\]
So dividing by \(\frac12 abc\) (all sides nonzero),
\[
\frac{\sin A}{a}=\frac{\sin B}{b}=\frac{\sin C}{c}=\frac{1}{2R}.
\]
Hence \(\frac{a}{\sin A}=2R\) and cyclic.

### Q3. Half-angle identity (Proof idea)
From double-angle:
\[
\cos\theta = 1-2\sin^2\frac\theta2 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \sin^2\frac\theta2 = \frac{1-\cos\theta}{2}.
\]
Also
\[
\cos\theta = 2\cos^2\frac\theta2 -1 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \cos^2\frac\theta2 = \frac{1+\cos\theta}{2}.
\]
Take square roots with sign determined by \(\theta/2\)'s quadrant when needed.

### Q4. Inverse Sine Principal Value (Proof idea)
Restrict \(x\in[-1,1]\). Function \(\sin\) on \([-\pi/2,\pi/2]\) is continuous and strictly increasing from \(-1\) to \(1\), therefore bijective onto \([-1,1]\). By inverse function theorem on monotone continuous maps, a unique inverse function exists there, denoted \(\arcsin\), with \(\sin(\arcsin x)=x\).

### Q5. Sum-to-Product (Proof idea)
Use addition formulas:
\[
\sin x+\sin y=\sin\left(\frac{x+y}{2}+\frac{x-y}{2}\right)+\sin\left(\frac{x+y}{2}-\frac{x-y}{2}\right).
\]
Applying \(\sin(u\pm v)=\sin u\cos v\pm\cos u\sin v\), terms combine to
\[
=2\sin\frac{x+y}{2}\cos\frac{x-y}{2}.
\]
Similarly for cosines,
\[
\cos x+\cos y=2\cos\frac{x+y}{2}\cos\frac{x-y}{2}.
\]

$A\cos(\omega t+\phi)$

## Round 12 Supplemental Terms

### Advanced Transform Identities

#### Term: Fejér Kernel
The **Fejér kernel** is
\[
F_n(t)=\frac1{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}D_k(t)=\frac1{n+1}\left(\frac{\sin\frac{(n+1)t}{2}}{\sin\frac t2}\right)^2.
\]

#### Term: Dirichlet Kernel
The **Dirichlet kernel** is
\[
D_n(t)=\sum_{k=-n}^n e^{ikt}=1+2\sum_{k=1}^n\cos(kt)=\frac{\sin((n+\tfrac12)t)}{\sin(t/2)}.
\]

#### Term: Fourier Series Convergence (Dirichlet)
If \(f\) is piecewise \(C^1\), its Fourier series converges to 
\((f(x^+)+f(x^-))/2\) at each point.

#### Term: Parseval Identity
For square-summable coefficients \(a_n,b_n\),
\[
\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)^2\,dx=\frac{a_0^2}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(a_n^2+b_n^2).
\]

#### Term: Gibbs Phenomenon
Near jump discontinuities, Fourier partial sums overshoot by approximately 9%, and oscillatory overshoot does not vanish as truncation order grows.

## Round 13 Supplemental Terms

### Operator-Based Trig Tools

#### Term: Hilbert Transform
The **Hilbert transform** of \(f\) is
\[
\mathcal H f(x)=\frac1\pi\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{f(t)}{x-t}\,dt.
\]

#### Term: Convolution with Kernel
If \((k\ast f)(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}k(x-t)f(t)\,dt\),
then many trig filters are realized as convolutions.

#### Term: Fractional Fourier Transform
The **fractional Fourier transform** \(\mathcal F_\alpha\) generalizes \(\mathcal F\) by rotating in the time-frequency plane by angle \(\alpha\).

#### Term: Angular Frequency
Angular frequency is \(\omega=2\pi f\), where \(f\) is linear frequency.

#### Term: Phase Modulation
A **phase modulation** signal has form
\[
A\cos(\omega t+\phi(t)).
\]
If \(\phi'\equiv c\), this is a shifted pure tone.

## Round 14 Supplemental Terms

### Fourier-Analytic Identities

#### Term: Fourier Series
A periodic function can be written as
\[
 f(x)=\frac{a_0}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^\infty(a_n\cos nx+b_n\sin nx),
\]
where \(a_n\) and \(b_n\) are Fourier coefficients.

#### Term: Dirichlet Kernel
\(D_n(x)=\sum_{k=-n}^{n}e^{ikx}=1+2\sum_{k=1}^{n}\cos(kx)\), used in trigonometric partial sums.

#### Term: Parseval Identity
For orthogonal trig basis,
\[
\frac1{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f(x)|^2dx=\frac{a_0^2}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(a_n^2+b_n^2).
\]

#### Term: Angular Frequency
If the period is \(T\), angular frequency is \(\omega=\frac{2\pi}{T}\), so waves are \(A\cos(\omega x+\phi)\).

#### Term: Harmonic Mean Value
For a pure tone represented as sine-cosine pair, the combined phase satisfies
\(A\cos(\omega x)+B\sin(\omega x)=R\cos(\omega x-\delta)\), with \(R=\sqrt{A^2+B^2}\).

## Round 15 Supplemental Terms

### Complex-Exponential Trig Synthesis

#### Term: Euler's Formula
For real \(x\),
\[
\exp(ix)=\cos x+i\sin x.
\]

#### Term: Exponential to Trig Conversions
\[
\sin x=\frac{e^{ix}-e^{-ix}}{2i},\quad
\cos x=\frac{e^{ix}+e^{-ix}}{2}.
\]

#### Term: De Moivre's Identity
For integer \(n\),
\[
\left(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta\right)^n=\cos(n\theta)+i\sin(n\theta).
\]

#### Term: Nth Roots of Unity
Solutions of \(z^n=1\) are
\[
z_k=e^{2\pi ik/n}=\cos\frac{2\pi k}{n}+i\sin\frac{2\pi k}{n},\;k=0,\dots,n-1.
\]

#### Term: Chebyshev Relation
The Chebyshev polynomial satisfies
\[
\cos(n\theta)=T_n(\cos\theta).
\]

## Round 16 Supplemental Terms

### Orthogonality and Harmonics

#### Term: Orthogonality of Sine-Cosine Basis
On \([-\pi,\pi]\),
\[
\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sin(nx)\sin(mx)\,dx=\begin{cases}0,&n\ne m,\\ \,\pi,&n=m\ne0,\end{cases}
\]
and similarly for cosine terms with mixed products integrating to zero.

#### Term: Parseval-Plancherel for Fourier Series
For square-integrable periodic \(f\),
\[
\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f(x)|^2dx=\frac{a_0^2}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(a_n^2+b_n^2).
\]

#### Term: Gibbs Phenomenon
A Fourier partial sum near a jump discontinuity overshoots by about 9% of jump size and converges with oscillations.

#### Term: Cesàro Summation
A divergent oscillatory trigonometric series may be assigned a summed value by averaging partial sums (Fejér means).

#### Term: Dirichlet Kernel Norm
The \(L^1\)-norm \(\|D_n\|_1\) grows like \(O(\log n)\), underpinning poor pointwise behavior of pointwise Fourier sums.

## Round 17 Supplemental Terms

### Trigonometric Kernel Identities

#### Term: Dirichlet Kernel
The Dirichlet kernel is
\[
D_n(\theta)=1+2\sum_{k=1}^n\cos(k\theta)=\frac{\sin\left((n+\tfrac12)\theta\right)}{\sin(\theta/2)}.
\]

#### Term: Fejér Kernel
Fejér kernel is
\[
F_n(\theta)=\frac1{n+1}\sum_{m=0}^{n}D_m(\theta)=\frac1{n+1}\left(\frac{\sin\frac{(n+1)\theta}{2}}{\sin\frac{\theta}{2}}\right)^2.
\]

#### Term: Spherical Harmonic Link
On the unit sphere, trig terms generalize to
\(Y_{\ell}^m(\theta,\phi)=N\,P_{\ell}^m(\cos\theta)e^{im\phi}\).

#### Term: Poisson Kernel
For \(|r|<1\),
\[
P_r(\theta)=1+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r^n\cos(n\theta)=\frac{1-r^2}{1-2r\cos\theta+r^2}.
\]

#### Term: Trigonometric Polynomial
A **trigonometric polynomial** is
\[
T(\theta)=a_0+\sum_{k=1}^n(a_k\cos k\theta+b_k\sin k\theta).
\]

## Round 18 Supplemental Terms

### Advanced Harmonic Function Expansions

#### Term: Poisson Summation Formula
For suitable \(f\),
\[
\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}f(k)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\hat f(n),\qquad
\hat f(\xi)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)e^{-2\pi i\xi x}\,dx.
\]

#### Term: Jacobi-Anger Expansion
\[
\exp\big(i z\cos\theta\big)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}i^n J_n(z)e^{in\theta},
\]
where \(J_n\) are Bessel functions.

#### Term: Spherical Trigonometric Form
A zonal harmonic expansion uses terms\(P_n(\cos\theta)\), while circular harmonics use \(\cos(n\theta),\sin(n\theta)\) as the 1D basis.

#### Term: Gibbs Constant
Near jump points, partial Fourier sums approach overshoot of approximately \\(rac{\text{jump}}{\pi^2}\sum_{k=1}^
fty\frac{\sin(k\theta)}{k}\) with limiting envelope known as Gibbs constant.

#### Term: Cesàro-Convergence of Fourier Series
If ordinary partial sums fail pointwise, Cesàro means\(\sigma_n f\) still converge at regular points to \((f(x^+)+f(x^-))/2\).

## Round 19 Supplemental Terms

### Periodic Convolutions and Kernels

#### Term: Circular Convolution
For\(2\pi\)-periodic functions,
\[
(f\ast g)(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x-t)g(t)\,dt.
\]

#### Term: Dirichlet Convolution of Coefficients
For arithmetic-like trig sequences, convolution of coefficient sequences is
\[(a\ast b)_n=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}a_kb_{n-k}.\]

#### Term: Fejér Mean
The \(n\)-th Fejér mean is
\[
\sigma_n(x)=\frac1{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^n S_k(x),
\]
where \(S_k\) is the \(k\)-th partial Fourier sum.

#### Term: Bochner Integrable Periodic Function
A function on \([-\pi,\pi]\) is Bochner integrable if it is measurable and
\(\int\|f(x)|dx<\infty\); its Fourier coefficients are well-defined and finite.

#### Term: Band-Limited Representation
A trigonometric series with \(a_n=b_n=0\) for \(|n|>N\) is **band-limited** to frequency \(N\).

## Round 20 Supplemental Terms

### Asymptotic and Distributional Harmonics

#### Term: Asymptotic Expansion of Oscillatory Integrals
For smooth \(\phi\) with nondegenerate stationary point \(x_0\),
\[
\int e^{i\lambda\phi(x)}a(x)dx\sim e^{i\lambda\phi(x_0)}\sum_{k\ge0}c_k\lambda^{-k-1/2},\quad \lambda\to\infty.
\]

#### Term: Sine Integral
\[
\operatorname{Si}(x)=\int_0^x\frac{\sin t}{t}\,dt.
\]

#### Term: Cosine Integral
\[
\operatorname{Ci}(x)=-\int_x^\infty \frac{\cos t}{t}\,dt.
\]

#### Term: Dirac Comb
The Dirac comb is
\(\Sha_T(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\delta(x-kT)\), connecting periodicity and Fourier sampling.

#### Term: Distributional Trigonometric Identity
In distribution sense,
\[\sin(ax)\delta(x-b)=\sin(ab)\delta(x-b),\qquad
\cos(ax)\delta(x-b)=\cos(ab)\delta(x-b).\]

## Round 21 Supplemental Terms

### Harmonic Signal Transformations

#### Term: Hilbert Transform
The Hilbert transform of \(f\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}\) is
\[
\mathcal Hf(t)=\frac1\pi\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{f(\tau)}{t-\tau}\,d\tau,
\]
shifting phase by \(\pm \pi/2\).

#### Term: Analytic Signal
For real signal \(x(t)\), analytic signal is
\(z(t)=x(t)+i\mathcal Hx(t)\), with instantaneous amplitude \(|z(t)|\).

#### Term: Fractional Period Shift
If \(f(t)=A\cos(\omega t+\phi)\), then 
\(\mathcal F_\alpha\{f\}\) corresponds to phase scaling by exponent matrix with fractional power \(\alpha\) (in fractional Fourier analysis).

#### Term: Harmonic Addition Formula
For coefficients \(a_n,b_n\):
\[
\sin\alpha\pm\sin\beta=2\sin\frac{\alpha\pm\beta}{2}\cos\frac{\alpha\mp\beta}{2},
\]
\[
\cos\alpha\pm\cos\beta=2\cos\frac{\alpha\pm\beta}{2}\cos\frac{\alpha\mp\beta}{2}.
\]

#### Term: Windowed Fourier Coefficient
For window \(w\), short-time Fourier coefficient is
\[
V_w f(t,\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)\overline{w(x-t)}e^{-2\pi i\omega x}\,dx.
\]

## Round 22 Supplemental Terms

### Harmonic Kernels and Special Trigonometric Systems

#### Term: Dirichlet Kernel
The **Dirichlet kernel** is
\[
D_n(\theta)=1+2\sum_{k=1}^n\cos(k\theta)=\frac{\sin\left(n+\frac12\right)\theta}{\sin\frac{\theta}{2}}.
\]

#### Term: Fejér Kernel
The **Fejér kernel** is the arithmetic mean of Dirichlet kernels:
\[
F_n(\theta)=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^n D_k(\theta)=\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\frac{\sin\frac{n+1}{2}\theta}{\sin\frac{\theta}{2}}\right)^2.
\]

#### Term: Jacobi-Anger Expansion
For \(z\in\mathbb C\),
\[
e^{iz\sin\theta}=\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}J_m(z)e^{im\theta},
\]
with Bessel functions \(J_m\).

#### Term: Bessel Function of the First Kind
The order-\(n\) Bessel function can be written as
\[
J_n(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\pi \cos(n\tau-x\sin\tau)\,d\tau.
\]

#### Term: Chebyshev Polynomial
For \(x=\cos\theta\),
\[
T_n(x)=\cos(n\theta).
\]

## Round 23 Supplemental Terms

### Convergence and Gibbs-Type Phenomena

#### Term: Trigonometric Polynomial
A finite sum
\[
\sum_{k=-N}^N c_k e^{ikx}
\]
is a **trigonometric polynomial**.

#### Term: Dirichlet Convergence Theorem
If \(f\in L^1[-\pi,\pi]\), then Fourier partial sums \(S_Nf(x)\) converge to \(\frac{f(x^+)+f(x^-)}2\) at jump points.

#### Term: Parseval Identity
For \(f\in L^2[-\pi,\pi]\), Fourier coefficients \(c_n\) satisfy
\[
\frac1{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f(x)|^2\,dx
=2\sum_{n=1}^\infty|c_n|^2+|c_0|^2.
\]

#### Term: Riemann--Lebesgue Lemma
For \(f\in L^1[-\pi,\pi]\), Fourier coefficients satisfy
\[
\hat f(n)\to0\quad \text{as }|n|\to\infty.
\]

#### Term: Gibbs Phenomenon
Near a jump discontinuity, Fourier partial sums overshoot by about \(9\%\) and do not converge uniformly to the midpoint profile.
